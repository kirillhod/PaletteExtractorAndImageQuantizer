How it works

  1. Build an alpha-weighted 5-bit color histogram.
  2. Extract colors or load a fixed palette, then map each pixel to its nearest entry.
  3. Dither optionally, or leave plain nearest-color mapping.
  4. Encode, decode, and compare the result before offering the file.

Extracting a palette

In Extract mode, the method trades speed, color balance, and smoothness. Colors sets the palette size from 2 to 256; fewer may come back when the image has fewer distinct colors. Alpha weights each pixel, and fully transparent pixels are ignored. Oklab is recommended; sRGB matches legacy tools. Median cut and Wu add two refinement passes.

Popularity
keeps the most frequent colors; best for flat art and pixel art.
Median cut
splits the color space by weighted error and refines the boxes.
Wu (balanced)
splits by color moments; the balanced default for photos.
Octree (fast)
merges a color tree quickly; lower quality.
K-means++ (max quality)
iterates clusters for the smoothest result; usually the slowest.

Mapping to a palette

Map replaces colors; it does not add them. Every pixel takes the closest palette color in the working space; exact ties go to the lowest index. The left half of the preview is the source and the right half the result; drag the divider or press O to compare. Dithering spreads the error to neighboring pixels so small areas can suggest colors the palette does not contain. Leave the transparent index at None to keep every color visible; choosing one removes every pixel assigned to that entry.

Palette order and preview

Export order changes, the color set does not. Sort changes export indices and the HEX list, not the color set. An indexed PNG keeps its visible colors but rewrites PLTE and the index map; the transparent index follows its color, and exact ties go to the lowest index. Click empty palette space or press Esc to clear the selection; with none, Pick fills HEX for Add. The preview button opens one full-screen strip on a neutral background; two gray presets and a color picker are remembered, and the preview never changes the export.

Hue order groups similar hues into bands and orders each band by lightness. Hue · grouped keeps a strict ramp. Hue · perceptual may reverse a band so neighboring bands meet at their closest ends. The direction button switches both Hue modes and Lightness between dark → light and light → dark.

Perceptual orders compare colors with Delta E (CIEDE2000) and change only the order and export indices. Their direction is fixed, so the direction button is disabled.

Smooth
keeps the light-to-dark flow and removes local jumps.
Color path
follows the closest colors from the darkest to the lightest.
Flow
keeps neighboring colors as close as possible without pinning the ends.

Picked colors keep a marker on the image while Edit is on. Drag a marker or focus it and use arrow keys; Esc cancels a drag. Extracted and imported colors get no marker until you pick them. Markers stay in the session and never enter exports.

Quality

Lower ΔE00 means a closer preview. Mean, p95, and max summarize the CIEDE2000 error between preview and source; the map runs from dark blue at 0 to red at 20+, and the worst-pixel list gives coordinates. Transparent pixels are skipped; contrast is not checked.

Shareable settings

The URL hash stores settings only. It stores mode, extractor, colors, working space, fixed palette, dither method, strength, serpentine, edge stop, alpha threshold, sort, and transparent index. The image, palette edits, locks, and quality results never enter it.

Saved session

The last session stays in this browser. It keeps the image, settings, palette, locks, order, selection, markers, edit mode, and undo history in IndexedDB. A URL with different settings takes precedence and re-extracts the palette. Forget saved session in the footer removes the stored copy.
